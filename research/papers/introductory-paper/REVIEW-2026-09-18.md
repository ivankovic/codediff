## The two open measurements

### R22 — RQ2 at 1000 pairs per language

`measure/overnight_rq1_refresh.sh` hardcoded `COUNT=140` (20 per language per LOC bucket); it now
takes `COUNT` from the environment, and `SKIP_FETCH=1` to leave the corpus on disk alone. At
`COUNT=1000` the sampler draws roughly 24,000 pairs over 24 languages, against the 2,922 the
committed numbers are measured on.

    cd research && COUNT=1000 SKIP_FETCH=1 ./measure/overnight_rq1_refresh.sh

`SKIP_FETCH=1` is sound here and only here: stage 2 draws a *fresh* sample by reading the
checkouts, so every pair it names resolves by construction, and stage 2b proves it before stage 3
measures anything. The decay problem the script was written for is about re-measuring an *old*
sample, which this is not.

Stage 3 is serial and timing-sensitive: every pair gets a hard 1-second budget, so a pair that
loses its CPU to something else becomes a spurious timeout. Cost, from the committed run's
44% timeout rate: roughly 10,000 timeouts at 1 s plus the rest, call it 4–6 hours, and nothing
else may run on the machine while it does.

**Done 2026-09-19.** 19,798 pairs over all 24 languages, every one resolving; 9,311 (47.0%)
completed inside the second. `\RqOnePairsAttempted` is 19,798, `\RqOneCodePct` 46.8 (was 54.5),
scripting 53.3 (was 83.9), config/data 45.3 (was 49.0); the 31-100 line bucket is 97.7 (was 92.7)
and 101-300 is 46.1 (was 25.3). The abstract, RA2, Table 6 and the time-budget figure all carry
the macros and moved with them.

It took two attempts. The first, on the evening of the 18th, measured the *gated* engine: since
2026-09-02 `resolve_forest` decomposes any pair above 600k cells instead of running APTED, and a
flat-container Myers pass predates even the August run, so the worker was reporting the product's
shortcuts as tree edit distance (77 of 84 August timeouts "completed", one in 0.5 ms). Fixed with
`Algorithm::AptedWholeTree`, which bypasses every shortcut, plus an `out_of_memory` status the
report counts as a non-completion; the second attempt is the one committed. The August numbers had
the Myers pass in them too, so the new rates are the first that measure what RQ2 says.
`data/rq1/PROVENANCE.md` has the full account.

### R48 — robustness over the whole Full corpus

Yes, but it is a day of machine time and it needs a producer that does not exist yet.

**What is missing.** `benchmark_diff_pairs` takes a CSV of `(repository, commit, path)` and reads
the blobs out of the checkouts, which is exactly the right shape. Nothing writes that CSV for the
whole corpus: `edit_shape_stats.py` walks all 578,783 code-file edits but emits only the aggregate
`data/corpus_stats/edit_shape.csv` (789 bytes). The per-edit list has to be emitted from that same
walk — a small change to a script that already visits every row.

**What it would cost.** Binning the 4,226 measured pairs in `data/performance/benchmark_*.csv` by
combined AST node count and weighting those bins by the corpus's own per-file node distribution
(Table 1: p50 334, p90 3,451, p99 23,948, max 905,004) gives **223 ms per diff, 36 CPU-hours** for
578,783. That is a floor: the sample has nothing above 31,623 combined nodes, so the top three
weight classes are all charged at the 10k–31k bin's mean. The unweighted mean over the same sample
is 717 ms, i.e. **115 CPU-hours**, and that is a ceiling because the sample is size-stratified and
deliberately over-weights large files. So: 1.5 to 5 days on one core, 5 to 15 hours on eight.

**What it would buy.** The Robust target is a ceiling of 905,004 AST nodes, and §8 already says the
ceiling is stated rather than exercised — the largest fixture is 198,406 nodes. The corpus's own
max is 905,004, so a full-corpus run is the only thing on this machine that would actually reach
the target. That is the argument for paying the day.

**Done 2026-09-19.** 444,199 modified code files (the 578,783 above counted creations and deletions
too, which have no pair to diff), 442,530 readable, **442,322 completed (99.95%)** with no panics,
median 12.2 ms, p99 2.1 s; the largest completed pair has 14,184,996 nodes across both sides,
fifteen times the ceiling. 96 pairs ran past 120 s and 112 past a 6 GB cap; the latter are 20
generated or embedded files plus one commit of a hand-written 40,000-line header, and given
24 GB one commit of each mostly completes or runs past five minutes. §8's Robustness paragraph,
its Limits, §9 and the abstract carry the macros (`RobustnessFull*`). It took five launches: an
OOM in the first took the Claude session down with it, and the restarts exposed a 2 MB harness
stack, an unbounded repository cache and a rename-parsing bug in the pair lister, all fixed
before the run that counts. `data/performance/PROVENANCE.md` has the full account.
